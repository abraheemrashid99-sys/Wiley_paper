\noindent\textbf{\sffamily Abstract}\par\smallskip
\noindent Large language models (LLMs) enable a semantic receiver to recover text from few channel symbols, because a model shared by both ends predicts the transmitted text. After deployment, however, content drifts, and the air time needed to keep the receiver's knowledge aligned is rarely counted. This article proposes doubly nested semantic communication (DN-SC), a token-level transceiver nested both in rate and in time. Each token is transmitted as a prefix of a 16-symbol codeword whose shells are power-controlled separately, so that the receiver can request further symbols only for uncertain tokens; a frozen GPT-2 model serves as the prior of a Bayesian beam-search decoder; and three receiver memories (context, logit bias, adapter slots) adapt the prior at three timescales. The return-link requests and every synchronized bit are converted into channel symbols. On a hierarchical drift stream with six seeds (AWGN, mean signal-to-noise ratio of 10\,dB), DN-SC attains 90\% token accuracy with 7.08 real channel symbols per token, against 9.23 for a frozen fixed-rate receiver with the same decoder: a reduction of 23.3\% with the requests included, observed on every seed, and of 27.9\% (95\% confidence interval 21.6--31.7\%) under free-running decoding, the measurement selected by the pre-specified rule. The context memory, which requires no synchronization, provides about three quarters of the reduction in prior cross-entropy achieved by the memories; relative to it, the shipped memories repay their synchronization cost only when one update serves more than approximately 6100 messages, an indicative figure because both costs are upper bounds.
