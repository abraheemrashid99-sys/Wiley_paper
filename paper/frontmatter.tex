\noindent\textbf{\sffamily Abstract}\par\smallskip
\noindent Large language models (LLMs) let a semantic receiver decode text from few channel symbols, because the model shared by both ends predicts what the transmitter is likely to say. After deployment the content drifts, and keeping the receiver's knowledge aligned costs air time that is rarely accounted for. This paper presents doubly nested semantic communication (DN-SC), a token-level transceiver nested in rate and in time. Every token is sent as a prefix of a 16-symbol codeword with exact per-shell power control, so that the receiver can request further symbols only for uncertain tokens; a frozen GPT-2 model is the prior of a Bayesian beam-search decoder; and three receiver memories---conversation context, a logit bias and low-rank adapter slots---adapt the prior at three timescales. Return-link requests and memory synchronization are charged in channel symbols. On a hierarchical drift stream with six seeds (AWGN, mean signal-to-noise ratio 10\,dB), DN-SC reaches 90\,\% token accuracy with 7.08 real channel symbols per token against 9.23 for a frozen fixed-rate receiver with the same decoder: 23.3\,\% fewer with the requests included, on every seed ($p = 0.031$), and 27.9\,\% fewer (95\,\% confidence interval 21.6--31.7\,\%) when decoded free-running. The free context memory supplies about three quarters of the memories' gain; the shipped memories repay their synchronization, relative to the context memory, only when one update serves more than about 6100 messages.
