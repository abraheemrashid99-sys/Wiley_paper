\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The sixth generation (6G) of mobile networks is expected to support services whose value lies in the meaning of the delivered content rather than in the exact reproduction of every bit \cite{itu2023,strinati2021,letaief2019,saad2020}. Exact reproduction is the problem formulated and solved by Shannon \cite{shannon1948}. Semantic communication addresses the level above it, identified by Weaver \cite{weaver1949}, and designs transmitter and receiver jointly around the information that the receiver actually requires \cite{gunduz2023,luo2022,kountouris2021}. Learned joint source--channel coding (JSCC) has shown that such systems degrade gracefully as the channel deteriorates and can outperform separate source and channel coding at short block lengths, both for text \cite{farsad2018,xie2021deepsc} and for images \cite{bourtsoulatze2019,kurka2021}.

Large language models (LLMs) contribute a further resource to this design space, namely an accurate statistical model of what a sender is likely to say. When both ends share such a model, the receiver can combine the likelihood of the received symbols with the model's prediction of the next token and decide on the most probable sentence, so that fewer channel symbols suffice for a given fidelity \cite{wang2024llmsc}. The context among tokens can be exploited in the same manner across modalities \cite{qiao2025token}, pre-trained language models have been used to rank the semantic importance of words \cite{guo2023importance}, and large AI models are being investigated as the knowledge base of semantic transceivers \cite{jiang2024largeai,xie2025largemodel,bariah2024}.

Two costs, however, are usually left outside the analysis of such systems. The first is the cost of keeping the shared knowledge aligned. After deployment the content changes, the topic of a conversation within minutes and the vocabulary of a domain over days, and a frozen receiver degrades accordingly. The receiver cannot adapt on its own, since only the transmitter has access to the source text; every update of the receiver's model must therefore be carried over the very link that the semantic codec is intended to economize. Existing work adapts or updates the knowledge of semantic transceivers after deployment \cite{zhang2023dynamic,zhang2026kbident,zhang2026kbupdate}, but for a language-model prior updated at several timescales the air time of the updates has not been set against the rate that they save. The second is the cost of adapting the rate. Incremental redundancy allows a receiver to request additional symbols only when necessary \cite{caire2001,jiang2022harq}, yet every request travels on a return link, and a token-level policy issues many requests.

This article studies both costs within a single system, termed doubly nested semantic communication (DN-SC). The system is nested in rate. Every token of the GPT-2 vocabulary \cite{radford2019} is mapped to a codeword of 16 real channel symbols whose coordinates are organized in shells; every transmitted shell is scaled to unit power, so that any prefix is a valid transmission and an additional shell extends the prefix of exactly the tokens for which it is sent. The receiver decodes with the language model as its prior and requests further shells only for the tokens whose posterior confidence remains below a threshold. The system is also nested in time. The receiver's prior is shaped by three memories that evolve at three rates, namely the context of the conversation (every message, without signaling), a logit bias in embedding space (every ten adaptation steps) and low-rank adapter slots of the language model \cite{hu2022lora} (every fifty steps, one slot per knowledge domain). The multi-frequency organization follows the nested-learning view of a model as a set of components updated at different rates \cite{behrouz2025}; in a transceiver, however, each update frequency carries a price in channel uses, and DN-SC accounts for that price exactly.

The evaluation is designed so that its outcome could contradict the proposal. Teacher-forced emulation, which scores each token given the true preceding tokens, is inexpensive but optimistic; the same received symbols are therefore also decoded free-running on a subset of the steps, and a decision rule fixed in advance determines which measurement is reported. The decoder of the refinement policy is selected on validation text only, by a pre-specified rule, and the confirmatory comparison relies on six seeds, paired bootstrap intervals and an exact seed-level test.

The main contributions of this article are summarized as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item A rate-nested token codebook with per-shell block power control is developed. Every transmitted block satisfies the power constraint with equality, which makes token-level incremental redundancy exact, and nesting causes no measurable loss at the prefix length that was examined (a channel-only token accuracy of 0.8885 against 0.8890 for a codebook trained for that length alone).
\item A time-nested receiver with an exact synchronization ledger is formulated. The context memory is built by the receiver from its own decisions, whereas the logit-bias and adapter memories are learned at the transmitter and shipped as quantized differences at their own periods; adapter slots retain one adapter per domain, and every shipped bit is recorded.
\item A complete cost model in channel symbols is derived, comprising the forward symbols, the return-link signaling of the refinement requests and the synchronization of the memories. It yields a net semantic rate, the traffic volume above which adaptation pays, and a separation benchmark that relates the prior cross-entropy to the number of symbols per token.
\item A pre-specified evaluation shows that, on a hierarchical drift stream, DN-SC requires 23.3\,\% fewer real channel symbols per token than a frozen fixed-rate receiver with the same decoder at 90\,\% token accuracy (27.9\,\% under free-running decoding), with the request signaling included and the synchronization accounted for separately. The analysis further shows that the free context memory carries most of the gain of the memories and that shipped adaptation pays only above a large traffic volume.
\end{itemize}
