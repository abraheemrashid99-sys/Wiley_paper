\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

This section first establishes the link-level behavior of the codec on the base corpus and the selection of the decoder on validation text. It then reports the primary endpoint on the drift stream, analyzes how the memories and the refinement policy contribute to it, and quantifies the cost of adaptation and of computation. A discussion of the scope and limitations of the findings concludes the section.

\subsection{Link-Level Performance on the Base Corpus}
\label{sec:results-base}

Table~\ref{tab:base} lists the free-running token accuracy and BLEU-4 of the codec on 200 test sentences of the base corpus over AWGN at 10\,dB, for four receiver priors and a greedy decoder, and Figure~\ref{fig:base} extends the comparison at 8 symbols per token to other SNRs and to Rayleigh block fading. The language prior lowers the rate required for a given fidelity at prefix lengths of 4 to 12 symbols; at 2 symbols per token GPT-2 is slightly below the other priors, and at 16 symbols all priors are essentially error-free. At 6 symbols per token, GPT-2 decides 85.7\,\% of the tokens correctly, against 73.7\,\% with token frequencies alone and 70.3\,\% without a prior, and BLEU-4 rises from 0.38 to 0.71. The last row of the table is a diagnostic that provides the receiver with the true preceding sentences of the document and thereby bounds the contribution of the fast memory (89.3\,\% at 6 symbols). Under Rayleigh block fading at 10\,dB and 8 symbols per token, the four priors reach 0.681 (none), 0.731 (unigram), 0.758 (GPT-2) and 0.785 (GPT-2 with true history). At 0\,dB the ordering reverses under both channels, and GPT-2 falls below the unigram prior at every prefix length (at 8 symbols per token, 0.119 against 0.196 over AWGN and 0.146 against 0.199 under Rayleigh fading), most likely because the greedy decoder then follows its own early errors. The operating point of this study is therefore an SNR of about 10\,dB.

Rate nesting causes no measurable loss at the prefix length that was examined: a codebook trained with the same budget for 8 symbols per token only reaches a channel-only token accuracy of 0.8890 at 10\,dB on 2000 held-out tokens, whereas the nested codebook, read at its 8-symbol prefix, reaches 0.8885. Before block power control, the codewords of the base corpus have an average power of 0.81--0.89 per symbol over the six prefixes, which block power control scales to exactly one per transmitted block.

\subsection{Decoder Selection}
\label{sec:results-decoder}

Table~\ref{tab:decoder} reports the selection on validation text. In the first stage, a first round of 4 symbols yields the lowest criterion (8.652 symbols per token), and a first round of 6 symbols (8.730) lies within 1\,\% of it and requires fewer rounds (3.01 against 4.01 per message), so that it is selected under the rule. Reopening loses in every case once its signaling is charged, because every token with shells left must then take part in every round's decision; at the lowest threshold its requests cost 2.3--4.2 bits per token at the entropy, against 1.5--2.5 without reopening. In the second stage, beam search of width 4 lowers the criterion by 4.9\,\%, above the 2\,\% bar, whereas width 2 (1.5\,\%) does not. For six of the eight candidates, including the selected one, the operating curve already exceeds 90\,\% at the lowest threshold of the validation grid ($\tau = 0.7$), so that the criterion is read there and constitutes an upper bound; the selected decoder reaches 94.5\,\% at that point and the greedy decoder with the same first round 91.8\,\%. The measured points nevertheless support the decisions: at their cheapest points, beam 4 is both cheaper and more accurate than the first-stage winner, and the bound of the 6-symbol first round can only decrease, so that this candidate ties with or outperforms the 4-symbol one.

The selected decoder, with beam width 4, a first round of 6 symbols and no reopening, requires 7.4\,\% fewer symbols than the greedy decoder starting from 2 symbols. The selection also shows that beam search benefits the fixed prefix far more than refinement: with width 4, the fixed prefix requires 17.7\,\% fewer symbols than with greedy decoding (9.049 against 10.994), because the beam removes much of the error propagation that refinement had previously compensated for. Every fixed-prefix reference in the remainder of this section is decoded with the same beam, so that all comparisons are made against this stronger baseline.

\subsection{Performance Under Semantic Drift}
\label{sec:results-drift}

Table~\ref{tab:primary} and Figure~\ref{fig:primary} present the primary endpoint. With its memories and selective refinement, DN-SC reaches 90\,\% token accuracy with 7.08 real channel symbols per token, compared with 9.23 for the frozen fixed-rate receiver. This is a reduction of 23.3\,\% with the return-link signaling charged at its entropy, of 28.3\,\% for the forward symbols alone and of 20.7\,\% with an explicit bitmap. Figure~\ref{fig:seeds} shows that the reduction occurs on every seed, between 10.5\,\% and 28.5\,\% (exact sign-flip test $p = 0.031$, the smallest value attainable with six seeds; Holm-adjusted over the three refinement receivers, $p = 0.094$), including the three seeds that had never been run before the protocol was fixed (20.6\,\%). Under free-running decoding on the audited steps, the reduction amounts to 27.9\,\% (95\,\% CI 21.6--31.7\,\%; 26.3\,\% with a bitmap), again on every seed, and to 25.9\,\% (95\,\% CI 11.0--33.2\,\%) on the unseen seeds alone. Seed~3 is the weakest on both measures ($-10.5$\,\% teacher-forced and $-9.1$\,\% decoded for real). These figures exclude the synchronization of the shipped memories, which is charged in Section~\ref{sec:results-cost}.

The savings of DN-SC are conservative. Its operating curve already exceeds 90\,\% at the cheapest point measured ($\tau = 0.5$) on four of the six seeds teacher-forced (90.3--93.1\,\%) and on the pooled curve decoded for real (92.1\,\%), so that its cost is read at that point, an upper bound of the cost at 90\,\%. The same holds for the context memory receiver on three of its four seeds and on its pooled real-decoding curve (90.3\,\%), and for the frozen receiver with refinement on one seed, whereas the frozen fixed-rate reference is always interpolated at 90\,\%.

Applied to the refinement policy, as implemented, the decision rule of Section~\ref{sec:setup-protocol} retains the teacher-forced curves: on the audited steps, real decoding requires $+3.4$\,\% (context memory), $+3.6$\,\% (DN-SC) and $+5.0$\,\% (frozen receiver) more forward symbols than the emulation. For the context memory and DN-SC, these are readings at the cheapest point, $\tau = 0.5$, at which real decoding is the more accurate of the two. Applied to the fixed-prefix receivers as well, which the wording of the rule also covers, the rule would not retain them: decoded for real, the fixed prefix requires $+9.7$\,\% (frozen), $+10.9$\,\% (context memory) and $+3.8$\,\% (DN-SC) more symbols than emulated on the same steps (10.37 against 9.45 for the frozen reference). Under that stricter reading, the real-decoding figures constitute the measurement. They yield the larger savings, because a fixed prefix suffers more from error propagation than the refinement policy, so that the teacher-forced figures quoted as the headline are the conservative ones; both are reported for every receiver. Figure~\ref{fig:operating} displays the pooled operating curves under free-running decoding. Refinement moves the curve of the frozen receiver to the left over the whole range it covers (87.7\,\% accuracy and above), whereas the memories of DN-SC raise the fixed-prefix curve at every rate and thereby leave less to refinement.

The stream runs read every refinement round off a single transmission of the message (Section~\ref{sec:setup-protocol}), so that a round carrying a subset of the tokens is sent at the gain of the whole message. To quantify the effect, the request policy was first emulated on 600 held-out sentences of AG News and TeleQnA (about 10{,}700 tokens), with the frozen prior and with the true preceding sentences as context, once with that gain and once with every refinement round scaled to unit power over the tokens it carries, as in~(\ref{eq:gain}). With the gain of the whole message, the refinement rounds at $\tau = 0.9$ carry on average 1.15 (no context) and 1.12 (context) times the permitted power per symbol, because the requested tokens have more energy in the later shells than the average token; individual rounds range from about 0.5 to 2.0 (5th to 95th percentile). Transmitting them at unit power raises the cost of refinement at 90\,\% token accuracy by 1.0\,\% and 0.8\,\%. Seed~0 of the stream was then repeated for the frozen receiver and DN-SC with every refinement round at unit power. The fixed prefixes are unaffected (9.089 and 10.253 symbols per token for the frozen receiver, teacher-forced and decoded for real). For the frozen receiver with refinement, the cost rises by 1.1\,\%, and its saving falls from 14.9\,\% to 13.9\,\% teacher-forced and from 17.8\,\% to 16.6\,\% decoded for real. For DN-SC with refinement, which requests fewer shells because its prior is stronger, the cost rises by 0.4\,\% (from 6.998 to 7.026 symbols per token), and its saving falls from 23.0\,\% to 22.7\,\% teacher-forced, while under free-running decoding it remains 27.9\,\%. Judged by this seed, the six-seed savings of Table~\ref{tab:primary} overstate the effect of the complete system by about 0.3 percentage points and that of refinement on the frozen receiver by about one point; the conclusions are unaffected.

\subsection{Contribution of the Memories and of Refinement}
\label{sec:results-memories}

The receiver memories and selective refinement both contribute to the saving, and their contributions overlap (Table~\ref{tab:primary}). At a fixed rate, the memories of DN-SC alone save 15.5\,\% teacher-forced (21.2\,\% decoded for real) without any return-link signaling, and refinement alone saves the frozen receiver 15.4\,\% (19.3\,\% decoded for real). On top of the memories, refinement adds a further 9.3\,\% teacher-forced and 8.4\,\% decoded for real (95\,\% CI 3.9--18.3\,\%); since the refinement readings of DN-SC are upper bounds, these values are lower bounds of its contribution. The better the prior, the less uncertainty remains for refinement to resolve.

Table~\ref{tab:decomposition} exposes the mechanism. The prior cross-entropy~(\ref{eq:metrics}) falls from 6.79 bits per token for the frozen receiver to 5.61 with the context memory and to 5.23 with all memories; on the four seeds shared by the three receivers, the context memory accounts for 76\,\% of that reduction. The shipped memories lower the prior by a further 0.37 bits per token and the selective cost at $\tau = 0.9$ by 0.12 symbols per token. Both intervals exclude zero, although with four seeds the seed-level test cannot go below $p = 0.125$. The signaling remains light: at the operating threshold, the requests of DN-SC cost 1.39 bits per token at the entropy (1.52 as a bitmap) over 2.84 rounds per message, against 1.62 bits and 3.07 rounds for the frozen receiver, in agreement with Proposition~3.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the components of the memories, obtained from pilot runs made with an earlier version of the code on a T4 GPU (greedy decoder, first round of 2 symbols, one to three seeds per receiver). These results are exploratory, and only the prior cross-entropy is reported, because it does not depend on the decoder of refinement except through the context. The context memory is indispensable, since DN-SC without it is 1.14 bits per token worse. Adapter slots help, as a single adapter dragged between the domains costs 0.10 bits per token. Shipping both gradient levels at one period with approximately the same synchronization budget (23.73 against 24.23\,kbit per step) and a single adapter costs 0.11 bits, and adding elastic weight consolidation \cite{kirkpatrick2017} does not help (0.12). \subsection{Cost of Adaptation and Computational Complexity}
\label{sec:results-cost}

The shipped memories are not free. DN-SC synchronizes 24.23\,kbit per adaptation step, which at the capacity of 10\,dB occupies about 14{,}000 real channel symbols, roughly 110 times the payload of one message at the target accuracy (about 127 symbols). Figure~\ref{fig:netrate} shows the net semantic rate~(\ref{eq:netrate}) of DN-SC relative to three references, on the four seeds shared by all receivers and with the signaling of refinement included. In accordance with Proposition~4, shipping pays against the frozen fixed-rate receiver once an update serves more than about 400 messages, and the saving then approaches 21.5\,\%. Against the frozen receiver with refinement, the crossover lies at about 1250 messages (asymptote 8.1\,\%), and against the context memory with refinement, which requires no synchronization, at about 6100 messages, with an asymptotic saving of only 1.8\,\%. The last comparison is indicative only, because on these seeds both refinement readings are mostly upper bounds taken above 90\,\% accuracy and are not taken at equal accuracy. In the stream itself, an update serves six messages. For a single link, the context memory with selective refinement is therefore the economical configuration, whereas shipped adaptation pays where one update serves many messages, such as the users of a cell or of a multicast group that share a domain.

The separation benchmark~(\ref{eq:separation}) relates these costs to the knowledge of the receiver. With the measured prior cross-entropies, it amounts to 3.93 real symbols per token for the frozen receiver and 3.02 for DN-SC, so that the memories lower the benchmark by 0.90 symbols per token. The measured costs, 9.23 symbols per token for the frozen fixed-rate receiver and 7.08 for DN-SC, lie at almost the same multiple of their benchmarks (2.35 and 2.34). On the base corpus, an idealized separate design that compresses each message by arithmetic coding with the same GPT-2 model and transmits it with a finite-block-length channel code in the normal approximation \cite{polyanskiy2010} delivers 42\,\% of the messages intact at 4 symbols per token and 96.5\,\% at 6 symbols (AWGN, 10\,dB, known SNR). This is a bound rather than a system, but it shows that the uncoded token-level codec does not outperform separation at a fixed rate and a known SNR (85.7\,\% token accuracy at 6 symbols, Table~\ref{tab:base}). The strengths of DN-SC lie instead in graceful degradation without a cliff, in a rate adapted per token by the receiver, and in an explicit account of the cost of keeping the knowledge aligned.

The receiver adds three small parameter sets to the frozen GPT-2 model (124,439,808 parameters): the codebook (213,248 parameters, trained once), the medium memory (771 values in the bit ledger, of which 770 are active in this configuration) and the adapter slots (147,456 parameters per slot). On a T4 GPU with one CPU thread, decoding one message of 19 tokens with the language prior took 193--199\,ms with greedy decoding, depending on the prefix length, against 3.4--3.7\,ms without a prior, so that the language model dominates the computational cost. A complete 600-step run of DN-SC, including 26 audited steps decoded for real at five thresholds and six prefix lengths with beam width 4, took 1.45--1.63\,h on one thread of the Linux CPU, against 0.99--1.11\,h for the frozen receiver. Because the transmitter must run the language model to learn the receiver's prior, adaptation costs transmitter computation in addition to air time.

\subsection{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:results-discussion}

Every result in this section is read from files that the code writes and that are publicly available, under a protocol that was fixed before the confirmatory runs and that allowed the results to contradict the proposal. Three findings are robust. A language prior with selective refinement saves channel symbols against a fixed-rate receiver with the same decoder, with the signaling included; the free context memory supplies most of what the memories gain; and shipped adaptation pays only above a traffic volume that a single link does not reach. The time nesting of the shipped memories is the weakest component, since the pilot ablation does not show that separating their periods matters and their measured benefit over the context memory is small.

The scope of the evidence is limited in several respects. Only one language model (GPT-2 with 124 million parameters) and one codebook were used; larger models will provide stronger priors at a higher cost of computation and of every adapter shipment. Six seeds yield $p = 0.031$ for each comparison of a refinement receiver with the frozen fixed-rate receiver, and the Holm-adjusted values are 0.094 at best, while the context memory receiver has four seeds and cannot reach a value below 0.125. The channel is simulated (AWGN on the stream) with perfect channel state information and a known message length; signaling and synchronization are charged at capacity and assumed error-free, and the delay of several rounds is counted but not modeled. On the stream, refinement and the receiver's context are emulated teacher-forced on every step and verified by real decoding on one step in 23, and the context is built from the emulated decisions. The six-seed stream runs transmitted refinement rounds at the gain of the whole message, which overstates the savings by up to about one percentage point as quantified above. The component analysis stems from pilot runs of an earlier code version with one to three seeds, and a stream with naturally occurring drift was not repeated with the final decoder. Finally, adapter slots are allocated greedily and never recycled, and a token is transmitted as an uncoded real vector without an outer channel code. The confirmatory runs were made on CPUs, and the pilot ablations and the latency measurement on a T4 GPU.
