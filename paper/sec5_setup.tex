\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Language Model, Codebook, and Corpora}
\label{sec:setup-model}

The frozen prior is GPT-2 \cite{radford2019} with 124,439,808 parameters, a vocabulary of $V = 50{,}257$ tokens and $D = 768$. The codebook has $M = 16$ real symbols per token, nested at the prefix lengths $\{2,4,6,8,12,16\}$ (shell widths $2|2|2|2|4|4$), with $H = 256$ hidden units and peak ratio $\eta = 4$; it has 213,248 parameters. It was trained once on the base corpus, the Europarl sample distributed with NLTK \cite{koehn2005} (14,006 sentences of 4--30 words, split 80/10/10 by document), for 2000 steps of 256 tokens, a quarter of them drawn uniformly from the vocabulary, with one SNR per token drawn uniformly in $[0,20]$\,dB and three prefix lengths per batch, using AdamW with a learning rate of $2\times10^{-3}$, 100 warm-up steps, cosine decay and gradient clipping at 1.0 (2194\,s on a CPU). The drift stream uses AG News \cite{zhang2015agnews} (news; four topic classes) and TeleQnA \cite{maatouk2023teleqna} (telecommunication questions on standards and research; five categories), with sentences of 4--30 words. An AG News document is a title and its description; a TeleQnA document is a question, its explanation and its answer. The stream draws its documents from the training split of each corpus and the validation set of Section~\ref{sec:setup-decoder} from the validation split, so no sentence is used twice.

\subsection{Stream, Channel, and Memories}
\label{sec:setup-stream}

The hierarchical stream visits AG News, TeleQnA, AG News and TeleQnA for $\tau_\mathrm{slow} = 150$ steps each (600 steps), with $N_\mathrm{c} = 6$ parallel conversations, a topic change every $\tau_\mathrm{med} = 30$ steps and $\kappa = 0.9$; a domain change ends every conversation. One run therefore carries 3600 messages of about 18 tokens. The channel is AWGN with the SNR process~(\ref{eq:snr}) at $\mu_\gamma = 10$\,dB, $\rho = 0.95$, $\sigma_\gamma = 4$\,dB, clipped to $[0,20]$\,dB; signaling and synchronization bits are charged at $C(10\,\mathrm{dB}) = 1.7297$ bits per real symbol. The context holds up to 48 tokens of the preceding messages of a conversation. The medium memory (two fusion weights and $\vect{u}\in\R^{768}$) learns with Adam at $10^{-3}$ and is shipped every $C_\mathrm{med} = 10$ steps; the slow memory has $K_\mathrm{s} = 2$ LoRA slots of rank 8 ($\alpha = 16$) on the query--key--value projection of the last six of the twelve layers, 147,456 parameters per slot, learns with Adam at $5\times10^{-4}$ and is shipped every $C_\mathrm{slow} = 50$ steps; the slot is chosen every $C_\mathrm{sel} = 10$ steps with $\delta = 0.05$ bits per token. Shipments use $b = 8$ bits per entry, and the loss~(\ref{eq:adapt-loss}) uses the prefix length of 6 symbols and one further prefix length per step. Table~\ref{tab:params} collects the parameters.

\subsection{Receivers}
\label{sec:setup-receivers}

Three receivers are compared on the same streams, codebook and decoder: the \emph{frozen} receiver, which decodes every message on its own with the unadapted prior (the per-message receiver of the literature); the \emph{context memory} receiver, which adds the fast memory only and therefore costs no synchronization; and \emph{DN-SC}, with all three memories. Each is evaluated at every fixed prefix length and with selective refinement. The reference of every relative change is the frozen receiver at a fixed prefix, which needs neither signaling nor synchronization. Component ablations---single-rate shipping at the same synchronization budget, elastic weight consolidation \cite{kirkpatrick2017}, shipping after every step, and DN-SC without slots, without context or with one period for both shipped memories---come from the pilot runs of an earlier code version and are reported as exploratory (Section~\ref{sec:results-ablation}).

\subsection{Decoder of Selective Refinement}
\label{sec:setup-decoder}

The decoder was chosen on validation text only, by a rule written into the repository before the selection was run. The candidates were the first round (2, 4 or 6 symbols per token), reopening of closed tokens (allowed or not) and the decoding of every pass (greedy, or beam search of width 2 or 4). The validation set holds one sentence, at a random position and with the true preceding sentences of its document as context, from each of 32 documents of the validation splits of AG News, TeleQnA and a public mirror of the News Category Dataset \cite{misra2022}: 96 messages and 1633 tokens, with the frozen prior, one AWGN realization per message and the SNR drawn from the stationary law of the SNR process (mean of the draws 9.8\,dB). The criterion is the number of real symbols per token for 90\,\% token accuracy, decoded for real, with the signaling charged at its entropy. Stage~1 compares the six greedy candidates, with candidates within 1\,\% counted as tied and the tie going to the one with fewer rounds per message; stage~2 adds beam widths 2 and 4 to the winner, and a beam is adopted only if it lowers the criterion by more than 2\,\%, since it costs computation. A beam search changes the receiver, so the fixed-prefix reference is decoded with the same beam.

\subsection{Measurement Protocol}
\label{sec:setup-protocol}

Every message is scored prequentially, with the parameters the receiver holds before the update of that step. Two measurements are taken. \emph{Teacher-forced emulation} on every step scores each token given the true tokens before it, which needs one pass of the language model per step and yields the complete operating curve (all prefix lengths, all thresholds); the decisions of the emulated policy at $\tau = 0.9$ are what the receiver writes into its context. \emph{Real decoding} on every 23rd step (26 steps, 156 messages per run; 23 is a prime, so the audit is never in phase with a shipping period) decodes the same received symbols free-running, round by round and with the configured decoder, for selective refinement at every threshold and for every fixed prefix alike. A decision rule fixed in advance determines which measurement is the result: the teacher-forced curves stand if, on the audited steps, real decoding needs at most 5\,\% more forward symbols at the target than the emulation for every receiver; otherwise the real-decoding figures replace them on both sides of every comparison. In the implementation, the adaptation loss~(\ref{eq:adapt-loss}) of a step reuses that step's channel realization; since an update reaches the receiver only after the step has been decoded, and the noise is independent across steps, this is equivalent in distribution to a realization drawn by the transmitter.

\subsection{Statistics and Runs}
\label{sec:setup-stats}

The frozen receiver and DN-SC were run on seeds 0--5 and the context memory receiver on seeds 0, 3, 4 and 5; its runs on seeds 1 and 2 were stopped by a restart of the machine and not repeated. Seeds 3--5 had never been run before the protocol was fixed. Seeds 0--2 ran on a Linux server CPU with one thread per run and seeds 3--5 on a Windows~11 laptop CPU with two threads per run, from code revisions whose stream runner and decoder are identical. The seed is the unit of replication. For real decoding, the operating curves of the audited steps are pooled over the seeds, every receiver is compared with the frozen receiver on the same seeds and steps, and the 95\,\% interval of each relative change is the percentile interval of 2000 paired bootstrap draws \cite{efron1993} of the audited steps within each seed, a draw in which a curve misses the target counting as an infinite cost. The seed-level test is an exact sign-flip permutation test on the per-seed costs; with six seeds its smallest attainable two-sided $p$-value is $2/2^6 = 0.031$, reached only when all seeds agree in sign, and with four seeds it is $0.125$. The tests are Holm-corrected \cite{holm1979} over the receivers compared with the frozen reference. Differences in prior cross-entropy between receivers are given with 95\,\% block-bootstrap intervals over steps (blocks of 25 steps) \cite{kunsch1989}, and a claim of no meaningful difference requires two one-sided tests \cite{schuirmann1987} with the 90\,\% interval inside $\pm0.05$ bits per token.
