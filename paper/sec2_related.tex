\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Learned semantic transmission of text}
\label{sec:related-jscc}

Deep joint source--channel coding (JSCC) replaces the separate source and channel codes by one learned mapping from content to channel symbols. For text, Farsad et al.\ \cite{farsad2018} encoded sentences with recurrent networks, and DeepSC \cite{xie2021deepsc} used a Transformer \cite{vaswani2017} with a fixed number of symbols per word, trained and evaluated on the Europarl corpus \cite{koehn2005}; its sentence lengths of 4--30 words became the common protocol of the field. For images, deep JSCC avoids the cliff effect of separate coding \cite{bourtsoulatze2019} and can deliver progressively refinable bandwidth-agile codes \cite{kurka2021}. Surveys and tutorials place these designs within the broader programme of semantic and goal-oriented communication \cite{strinati2021,gunduz2023,luo2022,kountouris2021}. Two properties of this line of work matter here. The encoder and decoder are trained jointly for a data distribution that is assumed fixed, and the number of channel uses per message is either fixed or chosen from a small set at the transmitter.

Rate adaptation at the receiver's request has been studied as semantic hybrid automatic repeat request (HARQ). Jiang et al.\ \cite{jiang2022harq} combined a learned sentence codec with retransmission and incremental knowledge, following the incremental-redundancy principle of classical HARQ \cite{caire2001}. Successive refinement \cite{equitz1991} and nested representations \cite{kusupati2022} supply the structural idea behind prefix codes whose leading part is useful on its own. DN-SC applies incremental redundancy per token rather than per message and makes the request depend on the posterior confidence of the receiver's language prior; it also charges the requests on the return link, which a token-level policy makes frequent.

\subsection{Language models in semantic communication}
\label{sec:related-llm}

Pre-trained language models enter semantic communication in three roles. As a measure of importance, they rank words so that power or bandwidth can be allocated to those that matter most \cite{guo2023importance}. As a knowledge base, large AI models supply the background knowledge of the transceiver and are argued to be a natural foundation for 6G semantic systems \cite{jiang2024largeai,xie2025largemodel,bariah2024}. As a prior, finally, the language model provides the probability of the next token given what has been decoded, which the receiver combines with the channel likelihood. Wang et al.\ \cite{wang2024llmsc} formulated this decoder for tokens as semantic units with a pre-trained LLM; Qiao et al.\ \cite{qiao2025token} used the context among tokens, through a large model, to save bandwidth. DN-SC belongs to this third group and takes the LLM-prior decoder of Ref.~\cite{wang2024llmsc} as its starting point. It adds the nested codebook with exact per-shell power control, token-level selective refinement, and memories that change after deployment.

\subsection{Knowledge mismatch and adaptation after deployment}
\label{sec:related-adapt}

A semantic receiver works because it shares knowledge with the transmitter, and that knowledge can diverge. Zhang et al.\ \cite{zhang2023dynamic} studied transceivers that must cope with a task-unaware transmitter and dynamic data, and knowledge-base mismatch between the two ends has been treated as a problem of its own \cite{kbmismatch2024}. In machine learning, adaptation to a changing data stream raises the problem of catastrophic forgetting \cite{kirkpatrick2017}; low-rank adaptation (LoRA) \cite{hu2022lora} makes updates of a large model small; and communication-efficient distributed learning compresses updates by quantisation \cite{alistarh2017,mcmahan2017}. The nested-learning paradigm \cite{behrouz2025} organises a model as components updated at different frequencies. In a transceiver, however, the receiver cannot compute its own updates without the source text, so every update has a price in air time that grows with its size and frequency. To our knowledge, this price has not been charged, at the level of individual bits and channel symbols, against the rate it saves; DN-SC does so for three memories at three timescales.
