\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

This section reviews the two lines of research on which DN-SC builds. The first concerns learned and LLM-enabled semantic transmission of text; the second concerns rate adaptation at the receiver's request and the alignment of transmitter and receiver knowledge after deployment. The section closes with a positioning of the present work.

\subsection{LLM-Enabled Semantic Communication}
\label{sec:related-llm}

Deep JSCC replaces separate source and channel codes by a single learned mapping from content to channel symbols. For text, Farsad et al.\ \cite{farsad2018} encoded sentences with recurrent networks, and DeepSC \cite{xie2021deepsc} employed a Transformer \cite{vaswani2017} with a fixed number of symbols per word, trained and evaluated on the Europarl corpus \cite{koehn2005}; its restriction to sentences of 4--30 words has been adopted by many later studies. For images, deep JSCC avoids the cliff effect of separate coding \cite{bourtsoulatze2019} and admits bandwidth-agile, progressively refinable codes \cite{kurka2021}. Surveys and tutorials place these designs within the broader program of semantic and goal-oriented communication \cite{strinati2021,gunduz2023,luo2022,kountouris2021}. In this line of work the encoder and decoder are typically trained jointly for a data distribution that is assumed to be fixed, and the number of channel uses per message is either fixed or selected from a small set at the transmitter.

Pre-trained language models enter semantic communication in three roles. As a measure of importance, they rank words so that power or bandwidth can be allocated to the words that matter most \cite{guo2023importance}. As a knowledge base, large AI models supply the background knowledge of the transceiver and are regarded as a natural foundation for 6G semantic systems \cite{jiang2024largeai,xie2025largemodel,bariah2024}; LLM-based text transceivers that dispense with a fixed knowledge base have been proposed \cite{chen2025llmsc}, and large models have been fine-tuned and compressed for multi-task semantic communication \cite{ni2025multitask}. As a prior, finally, the language model supplies the probability of the next token given the tokens already decoded, which the receiver combines with the channel likelihood. Wang et al.\ \cite{wang2024llmsc} built a text semantic communication system on this principle, with tokens as semantic units and a pre-trained LLM as the prior of the decoder, and Qiao et al.\ \cite{qiao2025token} exploited the cross-modal context among tokens through large models to save bandwidth. DN-SC belongs to this third group and adopts the LLM-prior decoder as its starting point.

\subsection{Rate Adaptation and Knowledge Alignment}
\label{sec:related-adapt}

Rate adaptation at the receiver's request has been studied as semantic hybrid automatic repeat request (HARQ). Jiang et al.\ \cite{jiang2022harq} combined a learned sentence codec with retransmission and incremental knowledge, following the incremental-redundancy principle of classical HARQ \cite{caire2001}. Successive refinement \cite{equitz1991} and nested representations \cite{kusupati2022} provide the structural basis of prefix codes whose leading part is useful on its own. DN-SC applies incremental redundancy per token rather than per message, conditions each request on the posterior confidence obtained with the language prior, and charges the requests on the return link.

A semantic receiver operates correctly only as long as its knowledge matches that of the transmitter. Zhang et al.\ \cite{zhang2023dynamic} studied transceivers that must cope with a task-unaware transmitter and dynamic data; Zhang et al.\ \cite{zhang2026kbident} test whether the knowledge bases of the two ends match and adapt them by a lightweight step when they do not; and cooperative update policies keep the knowledge bases of several semantic communication pairs current while limiting the overhead of the update \cite{zhang2026kbupdate}. In machine learning, adaptation to a changing data stream raises the problem of catastrophic forgetting \cite{kirkpatrick2017}, low-rank adaptation (LoRA) \cite{hu2022lora} keeps the updates of a large model small, and communication-efficient distributed learning compresses updates by quantization \cite{alistarh2017,mcmahan2017}. The nested-learning paradigm \cite{behrouz2025} organizes a model as components updated at different frequencies. In a transceiver, however, the receiver cannot compute its own updates without the source text, so that every update incurs a price in air time that grows with its size and frequency.

DN-SC differs from the work above in that it charges every update of a language-model prior, at three timescales, bit by bit and in channel symbols, against the rate saved by that update, and thereby obtains the traffic volume above which adaptation pays.
